Under Deck Ceiling Options for a Dry Deck

Most under deck ceilings are built to catch rain that falls through the deck. On an AmeriDex deck, rain sheds off the top, so the ceiling below is a finish you choose, not the thing keeping you dry.

Under deck ceiling checklist

  • ✓Use a vented vinyl or PVC soffit that allows airflow. Wood ceilings are not recommended.
  • ✓Provide at least 1 1/2 inches of continuous ventilated screen at the fascia, or use a vented soffit throughout.
  • ✓Outdoor space below only. Never install the system over interior living space.
  • ✓Plan the deck slope at framing: 1/8 inch per foot, away from the house.
  • ✓On decks with boards running away from the house, the framing leaves room for a gutter at the house. Plan the ceiling around it.
  • ✓Open sides can still get wind-driven rain. Plan furniture, lighting, and screens with that in mind.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put a ceiling under an AmeriDex deck?

Yes. Use a vented vinyl or PVC soffit that allows airflow; wood ceilings are not recommended. Provide at least 1 1/2 inches of continuous ventilated screen at the fascia, or use a vented soffit throughout.

Do I need a ceiling to keep the space under my deck dry?

No. On an AmeriDex deck the boards lock onto the Dexerdry seal, so rain sheds off the top of the deck instead of dripping through the gaps. A ceiling below is a finish choice. Many owners leave the joists open.

How is this different from an under deck ceiling drainage system?

Under deck ceiling and tray systems are installed below the joists to catch water that has already come through the deck boards and route it to a gutter. With AmeriDex, water stays on top of the boards, so there is no trough under the joists that can collect debris or hold standing water.

Why not use a wood ceiling?

The AmeriDex installation instructions do not recommend wood ceilings under the deck. Use a vented vinyl or PVC soffit so air can move through the space between the deck and the ceiling.

Can I build this over a room inside the house?

No. Never install the system over interior living space. It is made for decks, porches, and balconies with outdoor space underneath, such as a patio, an outdoor bar, or storage.

Will the space under the ceiling stay dry on a windy day?

The deck surface keeps rain from dripping through from above. An open-sided space may still get wind-driven rain, so plan furniture, lighting, and screens with that in mind.
