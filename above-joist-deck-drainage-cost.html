How Much Does an Above-Joist Deck Drainage System Cost?

Real numbers, not a routing form. Here is the per-foot price, what it covers, what a deck your size works out to, and a calculator you can run right now.

AmeriDex runs about $8.00 per lineal foot of deck board, and that price includes both the cellular PVC decking and the Dexerdry seal. For a typical deck that works out to roughly $16.88 per square foot in materials.

Pricing shown is material cost only, is a typical figure, and can vary by color, length, accessories, and dealer. Labor is quoted separately. Request a written quote for an exact number.

What an above-joist deck drainage system costs by deck size

Above-joist drainage is priced by the lineal foot of board, not by the square foot, because the board is the drainage system. A 5.5 inch board face plus the 3/16 inch gasket gap covers 5.6875 inches of deck, so one square foot of deck takes about 2.11 lineal feet of board. What is included in that price

The $8.00 per lineal foot figure is a materials number, and it covers the decking and Dexerdry seal for planning purposes:

  • Cellular PVC deck boards in the color and length you choose
  • The Dexerdry TPE seal between every board, which is what makes the surface watertight
  • Fastener type, quantities, and pricing must be confirmed in the written quote; do not assume they are included in this planning allowance.

What the price does not cover:

  • Labor, which your builder or dealer quotes separately
  • Framing: joists, beams, posts, footings, and the ledger
  • Railing
  • Trim and fascia
  • Permits and inspections

Because the drainage is part of the deck surface, there is no separate drainage product on the invoice. See how the two-part system goes together.

How above-joist and below-joist drainage costs differ

Compare the same project scope before comparing prices. Surface-integrated systems combine decking and drainage; separate above-joist systems add a layer beneath the boards; below-joist systems collect water under the framing.

Separate above-joist systems can also intercept water above the covered framing. Installation location, access, accessories, and finishing details affect the quote. No generic labor or lifetime-savings claim replaces a project-specific takeoff.

Can it go on an existing deck?

Here is the honest answer. AmeriDex is installed as the deck surface, with the seal placed between every board as the boards go down, so it is a new-build or re-deck system rather than something that can be slipped in later.

During a re-deck, where the old boards come off and new boards go on, it can often be installed on your existing joists, provided a builder confirms framing condition, spacing, drainage pitch, flashing, fastening, and all required installation details. That is a common and cost-effective path: you are already paying for new decking, and the drainage comes with it.

What it cannot do is go underneath a deck whose boards stay in place. If that is your situation, a below-joist trough or membrane retrofit is the category to look at, with the trade-off that the framing keeps getting wet. Not sure which one you are? Send your dimensions through the quote form and a dealer can assess whether your framing can carry a re-deck.

Is above-joist drainage cheaper than a below-joist system?

Not necessarily. Compare complete, equivalent project scopes. AmeriDex integrates drainage at the deck surface.
