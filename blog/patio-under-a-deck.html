Can You Build a Patio Under a Deck?

Yes. A patio under a gapped deck gets rained on from above. Seal the surface so rain sheds off the top, and the space below becomes a dry outdoor room you can actually use.

Published November 7, 2026

Homeowners ask this all the time: can the space under an elevated deck become a real patio, not a muddy storage pit that drips every time it rains? The short answer is yes, if the deck surface itself stops water from falling through.

The short answer

A patio under a deck stays dry when rain sheds off the top of the boards instead of dripping through the gaps. AmeriDex boards lock onto the Dexerdry seal so water runs to the drip edge, and the space below is a dry space for dining, lounging, or storage.

Why a patio under a gapped deck gets wet

Standard deck boards are installed with expansion gaps. Those gaps act like slot drains. Rain falls through onto furniture, cushions, and the patio surface. People sometimes hang a tray under the joists to catch that water. The tray can divert runoff, but the joists still get wet every time it rains. For a patio you want to sit under, the better fix is to seal the surface so water never drops through in the first place. See why water drips through deck boards and how a dry space under a deck works.

What actually has to change

Three things turn the under-deck area into a usable patio:

  • A sealed surface. AmeriDex boards lock onto the Dexerdry seal between every board so rain stays on top and runs off the ends.
  • Slope. Pitch the deck at least 1/8 inch per foot away from the house so water keeps moving.
  • Overhang and drip edge. Boards overhang the framing by at least 1 3/8 inch, with the drip edge about 3/8 inch outside the frame. Cut the drip edge 3/4 to 1 inch from the end and 7/16 to 1/2 inch deep so water drops clear instead of wicking back.

Joists stay at 16 inches on center or less. Flash the ledger where the deck meets the house (see our deck ledger flashing guide). A perimeter gutter is not required; water sheds off the drip edge. Open sides can still get wind-driven rain, so plan for that.

Second-story decks

A patio under a second-story deck is the same idea: keep rain on top of the boards. Read second-story deck waterproofing for how that layout fits together.

Patio under deck planning checklist

1

Confirm the pitch

Slope at least 1/8 inch per foot away from the house. Flat framing with peaks or valleys leaves water sitting on the surface.

2

Plan overhang and drip edge

At least 1 3/8 inch past the framing, drip edge about 3/8 inch outside the frame, cut 3/4 to 1 inch from the end and 7/16 to 1/2 inch deep.

3

Flash the ledger

Water that gets behind the ledger can damage the house. Follow the flashing details before the decking goes on.

4

Keep furniture back from the edge

Rain sheds past the drip edge, so that outer strip of ground gets wet. Pull tables and lounges inward into the dry space.

5

Decide on a soffit finish

Optional: a vented vinyl or PVC soffit with at least 1 1/2 inches of continuous ventilated screen at the fascia. Wood ceilings are not recommended.

6

Never over interior living space

AmeriDex is for outdoor decks, porches, and balconies. Do not install it over interior living space.

7

Gutters only where the layout needs them

If boards run away from the house, leave room for a gutter at the house. Wide decks need a seam or center-board gutter. The outer drip edge does not require a perimeter gutter.

Do

  • Slope 1/8 inch per foot away from the house
  • Overhang at least 1 3/8 inch with a drip edge
  • Keep patio furniture back from the drip edge
  • Use a vented vinyl or PVC soffit if you finish the ceiling
  • Water test the finished deck

Don't

  • Expect a gapped deck to keep a patio dry
  • Hang your hopes on a below-joist tray alone
  • Install over interior living space
  • Put furniture in the wet strip at the outer edge
  • Use a wood ceiling under the deck

New build vs re-deck

On a new deck you can plan pitch, overhang, ledger flashing, and any house gutter from day one. Boards come in 12, 16, and 20 foot lengths. On an existing deck, a re-deck works if the framing already meets the pitch and overhang requirements. Old boards come off; AmeriDex boards and the Dexerdry seal go on. What you can do with the dry space

Once rain stops dripping through, the area under the deck is outdoor square footage you can furnish:

  • Patio dining with a table that stays dry when it rains
  • A lounge with sofas or chairs pulled back from the drip edge
  • Dry storage for cushions, bikes, or kayaks
  • A screened porch under the deck

Frequently asked questions

Can you build a patio under a deck?

Yes. On a standard gapped deck, rain drips through the board gaps onto the patio below. With AmeriDex, the boards lock onto the Dexerdry seal so rain sheds off the top of the deck past the drip edge, and the space below stays dry for a patio.

Why does a patio under a regular deck get wet?

Deck boards are installed with expansion gaps that act like slot drains. Rain falls through those gaps onto everything below, including furniture and the patio surface. A tray hung under the joists can catch some of that water, but the joists still get wet.

Do I need a perimeter gutter for a patio under an AmeriDex deck?

A perimeter gutter is not required. Water normally sheds off the drip edge at the outside of the deck. Keep patio furniture back from that outer strip, because it gets wet. Gutters are needed at the house when boards run away from the house, and at seams or center boards on wide decks.

Can I add a dry patio under my existing deck?

Often, as a re-deck, if the existing framing meets the pitch and overhang requirements. The old boards come off and AmeriDex boards with the Dexerdry seal go on. Never install the system over interior living space.

Can I finish the ceiling under a patio deck?

Yes. Use a vented vinyl or PVC soffit that allows airflow. Provide at least 1 1/2 inches of continuous ventilated screen at the fascia, or use a vented soffit throughout. Wood ceilings are not recommended.
