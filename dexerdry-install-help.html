What is Dexerdry?Dexerdry is a flexible weather strip, called a flange, that fits into the side grooves of grooved deck boards to create a DRY ZONE under your deck.product facts

Dexerdry is a flexible weather strip, called a flange, that fits into the side grooves of grooved deck boards to create a DRY ZONE under your deck.

Rain sheds off the top of the deck instead of dripping through the gaps between boards, which helps keep the area under the deck, porch or balcony dry. It comes on a roll and is cut to the length of each board. It is not rubber; it is a thermoplastic material.

Is Dexerdry a channel or trough under the joists?No, Dexerdry is not installed under the joists; it sits on top of the framing, in the grooves between the deck boards.product facts

No, Dexerdry is not installed under the joists; it sits on top of the framing, in the grooves between the deck boards.

Water sheds off the top of the deck and leaves at the board ends, past the drip edge, and through planned drainage gaps into gutters. Nothing hangs below the joists.

Where does the water go?Water runs across the top of the boards and sheds off the overhanging ends past the drip edge.product facts

Water runs across the top of the boards and sheds off the overhanging ends past the drip edge.

Where a deck needs it, water also drops through small drainage gaps into gutters: at the house on decks with boards running away from the house, at seams and center boards on wide decks, and at the picture frame gap. Slope the deck 1/8 inch per foot away from the house so water keeps moving.

Use the Dexerdry profile made for your deck board brand, because every manufacturer's groove is a different size.

The correct flange fits snug in the groove; the wrong one fits loose and leaves a gap. New profiles are added over time, so check dexerdry.com for the profile that matches your boards.

What is the drip edge for?The drip edge is a cut under each board end that stops water from wicking back under the board and into the grooves.drip edge

The drip edge is a cut under each board end that stops water from wicking back under the board and into the grooves.

Water naturally creeps along surfaces (capillary action). The drip edge breaks that path so water drops off the end instead. It is an essential step.
